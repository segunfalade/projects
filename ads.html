04 / A repeatable practice

A 15-minute analysis loop

You’re not trying to collect clever lines. You’re training yourself to spot the strategic choices that make an offer easier to understand, trust and act on.

Ask these six questions

  • ✓Who is this for, right now? What situation, awareness level or trigger is the copy speaking to?
  • ✓What change is being sold? What will the person be able to do, decide, experience or stop struggling with?
  • ✓How is an intangible outcome made visible? Look for a process, milestone, artifact, scenario, behavior shift or client evidence.
  • ✓Why believe it? Identify the proof type, its context and what it does—and does not—support.
  • ✓What hesitation is handled? Notice fit filters, scope, access, time, risk reversal and the cost of doing nothing.
  • ✓What is the next step? Is the CTA clear, proportionate to the promise and low-friction enough for this moment?

06 / Your differentiator

Translate the lesson for intangible outcomes

When a result can’t be reduced to a neat metric, specificity can come from other places. Look for what makes the change observable and credible.

Make the result visible

Describe the before-and-after in real life

What decision gets easier? What conversation changes? What repeated problem stops consuming attention? What can the client do differently?

Show the path

Name the mechanism, not just the aspiration

Make the work tangible through the diagnostic, method, feedback, facilitation, milestones or artifacts that help create the change.

Earn belief honestly

Use proof that fits the claim

Ground the promise in specific case context, client language, work samples or observed shifts. Don’t invent numbers—or imply certainty the evidence cannot support.

07 / Turn study into visibility & pipeline

One example can create two useful assets

LinkedIn post starter

“I studied [example] today. The persuasive move wasn’t [surface detail]—it was [strategic choice]. For experts selling [intangible change], that matters because [reason]. One way to make that promise more concrete is [observable process, proof or outcome].”

Add your analysis and client context. Don’t simply repost someone else’s copy.

Respectful audit-outreach bridge

“I noticed [specific element] on your [page/post]. A first-time prospect may still be wondering [one unanswered question about the result, process or proof]. I mapped a small copy opportunity using the evidence you already have. Would it be useful if I sent the one-page audit?”

Make the observation genuinely specific, and ask permission before sending the audit.
