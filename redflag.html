05 / A fair, repeatable method

Diagnose the friction, then test the repair

Use the same questions on every specimen. This keeps “bad copy” from becoming a synonym for “not my style.”

Copy-forensics checklist

  • 1Five-second read: can I tell who this is for, what is offered and what to do next?
  • 2Point to the line: quote the smallest excerpt that creates confusion or doubt.
  • 3Name the failure mode: audience, promise, mechanism, proof, offer, sequence, CTA, voice, accessibility or production.
  • 4State the reader effect as a hypothesis: “A first-time visitor may wonder…”—not “This definitely loses sales.”
  • 5Find the smallest repair: clarify one audience, outcome, process, proof point or next step.
  • 6Check the context: source traffic, stage of awareness, device, offer, audience language and brand voice may change the diagnosis.

Coach / consultant lens

PROMISE
Is the transformation only aspirational?Can the reader picture a decision, behavior, conversation, milestone or experience?
PROCESS
Can they imagine how the service works?Is the method, scope, cadence or first step concrete enough?
PROOF
Does the evidence support the claim?Is the testimonial specific and contextual, or just “amazing experience”?
CTA
Does the next step ask for too much trust?Does “book a call” explain what the call is for and what will happen?
ETHICS
Are claims and urgency honest?Don’t solve vague copy by inventing metrics, scarcity or guaranteed outcomes.
Separate the problem

Copy, offer, or business model?

If the promise is unclear because the offer itself is undefined, a headline rewrite alone will not fix it.

Separate observation from outcome

“Unclear to me” ≠ “doesn’t convert”

You can diagnose comprehension and friction. Conversion performance needs evidence such as analytics, tests or customer research.

Keep the craft

Repair the minimum necessary

Keep the owner’s point of view and voice where they help. Clarity doesn’t mean sanding every personality off the page.

Turn a critique into a client-friendly observation

“I noticed [specific strength] on your [page/ad]. One question a new prospect may still have is [specific question]. A small copy change could make [process, proof or outcome] easier to understand. I mapped it out in a short audit—would you like me to send it?”

Practice with care: critique the artifact, not the person. Don’t publicly roast a coach or consultant you may want to work with. Keep private inbox material private; only use publicly available examples or examples shared with permission. Don’t republish full copyrighted copy.
